# Personal Loan Reporting Error on Your Credit Report: What to Do

**Important:** CashPath is not a lender, loan servicer, credit reporting company, credit-repair company, or law firm. It cannot change a credit report, remove accurate information, or guarantee a credit-score result. CashPath may receive referral compensation; see the [Advertiser Disclosure](/p/advertiser-disclosure). This guide provides general U.S. consumer education.

## Short Answer

If a personal-loan account you recognize is reported inaccurately, identify the exact field that is wrong, collect records that support your position, and dispute the inaccurate information with both the credit reporting company and the company that furnished the information.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's guidance, last modified September 2, 2026, says consumers have the right to dispute inaccurate credit-report information with both parties.

Examples of errors the CFPB tells consumers to look for include:

- an account incorrectly reported as late or delinquent;
- a closed account reported as open;
- an incorrect current balance;
- an incorrect date of last payment;
- an incorrect date opened or date of first delinquency; and
- the same debt listed more than once.

If the account is **not yours at all**, treat that as a possible identity-theft issue rather than an ordinary reporting error.

If the information is **accurate but negative**, do not file a dispute merely because the information hurts your credit. Accurate negative information generally cannot simply be removed.

## First Decide Which Problem You Actually Have

Before sending a dispute, put the problem into one of three buckets.

**Recognized account, wrong information.**
You know the loan is yours, but a balance, payment status, date, ownership field, or account status appears inaccurate. This page covers that problem.

**Unrecognized account or inquiry.**
You do not recognize the loan, creditor, or inquiry. That may point to identity theft, a mixed credit file, or another identity error. Use the CashPath guide [Someone Opened a Loan in My Name](/p/someone-opened-a-loan-in-my-name) and review current CFPB identity-theft steps.

**Accurate but negative information.**
The late payment, delinquency, or other negative item is correct. The CFPB warns that accurate negative information generally cannot be removed simply because it is harmful. Be cautious of a company promising to erase accurate information for a fee.

Getting the category right prevents a factual dispute from turning into an unsupported deletion request.

## Get the Credit Report That Contains the Error

Work from the actual report, not only a credit-score app summary or lender email.

Save a copy showing:

- the credit reporting company;
- the account name;
- account number as displayed;
- status;
- current balance;
- payment history;
- important dates; and
- the exact field you believe is inaccurate.

If the same error appears on more than one credit report, record each report separately. An error corrected on one file should not be assumed to have already disappeared from another.

If the issue is specifically that a paid-off loan still shows the wrong balance or status, also see [What Happens to Your Credit Report After Paying Off a Personal Loan?](/p/what-happens-to-credit-report-after-paying-off-personal-loan).

## Gather Documents Before You Dispute

A good dispute identifies a specific factual problem and supports it with records.

Depending on the error, useful documents can include:

- monthly loan statements;
- bank records showing a payment cleared;
- payment confirmations;
- payoff confirmation;
- account-closing correspondence;
- loan agreement or servicing records;
- written correspondence with the creditor or servicer; and
- copies of the credit reports showing the disputed field.

Do not send original documents that you cannot replace.

Create a simple evidence note for yourself:

- **Reported:** ____________________
- **Should be:** ____________________
- **Why:** ____________________
- **Supporting document:** ____________________

That is much stronger than writing only, "This account is wrong."

## Dispute With the Credit Reporting Company

The CFPB recommends disputing inaccurate or incomplete information with the credit reporting company that issued the report.

Your dispute should clearly identify:

- who you are;
- the account involved;
- the exact information you dispute;
- what you believe the correct information is;
- why the current reporting is inaccurate; and
- which documents support your position.

Use the reporting company's current dispute instructions. Keep copies of what you submit and any confirmation number or mailing record.

A credit reporting company may decide that a dispute is frivolous or irrelevant if it does not provide enough information to investigate. Specificity matters.

## Also Dispute With the Furnisher

A **furnisher** is the company that supplied the account information to the credit reporting company. For a personal loan, that may be the creditor, servicer, or another company reporting the account.

CFPB guidance recommends disputing the inaccurate information with the furnisher as well as the credit reporting company.

The furnisher dispute should identify the same exact error and include supporting documents.

The CFPB says furnishers generally must investigate and respond to a direct dispute within 30 days after receiving it. Treat that as general federal guidance, not a promise that every dispute will be resolved on a particular day. If the investigation shows that the furnisher supplied incorrect information, or the information cannot be verified as required, the furnisher may have duties to update or remove the information and notify credit reporting companies that received it.

Do not assume the company named on your CashPath request flow is the furnisher. CashPath is a referral service and does not service personal-loan accounts.

## How Long Can a Credit-Report Dispute Take?

The CFPB says a credit reporting company generally must investigate a dispute within 30 days after receiving it.

In some circumstances, the investigation period can extend to 45 days, including when additional relevant information is submitted during the investigation or in certain disputes connected with a free annual credit report.

The reporting company generally provides results after the investigation, and an updated report may be included when the dispute causes a change.

Treat those as general federal timing rules, not a promise that every case will be resolved on a particular day.

## What to Do After You Receive the Results

When the response arrives, compare it with the original error.

Check:

- Was the disputed field changed?
- Does the account now show the correct balance, status, or date?
- Did the furnisher or reporting company explain why it found the information accurate?
- Does the same error remain on another credit report?
- Did you receive instructions for further action if you disagree?

Save the response with the original evidence.

If a furnisher or credit reporting company continues to report information you believe is inaccurate after a properly supported dispute, review the CFPB complaint process and consider getting qualified legal advice for your circumstances.

## Do Not Pay for a "Secret" Dispute Method

The right to dispute inaccurate information is not a product you need to buy.

The CFPB's September 2, 2026 guidance says there is no reason to pay someone else merely to dispute inaccuracies for you because consumers already have that legal right at no cost.

Be skeptical of claims such as:

- "We can delete any late payment."
- "We have a special letter the bureaus must obey."
- "Pay us and your score is guaranteed to rise."
- "All negative information can be erased."

A legitimate dispute is about **accuracy**, not a guaranteed score outcome.

## A Corrected Error Does Not Guarantee a Specific Credit-Score Change

Credit scoring depends on the complete credit file and the scoring model used.

Correcting inaccurate information can make the report more accurate, but CashPath cannot tell you:

- whether a score will rise;
- how many points it might change;
- how quickly a score provider will update; or
- whether a future creditor will approve an application.

Avoid content that turns a legal right to accuracy into a promise of credit improvement.

## If the Account Is Not Yours

An unfamiliar personal-loan account is not merely a balance typo.

Do not send money or sensitive information to an unknown caller who claims the account is yours.

Instead:

- preserve the report showing the unfamiliar account;
- review the CashPath identity-theft guide;
- follow current IdentityTheft.gov and CFPB recovery steps;
- consider fraud alerts or security freezes as appropriate; and
- contact the legitimate company using independently verified contact information.

This keeps ordinary dispute steps from obscuring a possible identity-theft problem.

## If the Information Is Accurate but You Missed a Payment

Do not dispute an accurate late payment simply because you want it removed.

If you are struggling with repayment now, contact the actual creditor or servicer and ask what options, if any, are available under your agreement.

For lifecycle guidance, read [What Happens If You Miss a Personal Loan Payment?](/p/what-happens-if-you-miss-a-personal-loan-payment).

If a debt collector contacts you, verify the company and debt before sharing sensitive payment information.

## Practical Error-Dispute Checklist

- I am looking at the actual credit report containing the error.
- I recognize the account as mine.
- I identified the exact field that is inaccurate.
- I collected statements, payment records, payoff records, or other supporting documents.
- I disputed the error with the credit reporting company.
- I disputed the same error with the furnisher.
- I saved copies and confirmation records.
- I checked the investigation result against the original error.
- I reviewed other reports if the account is reported by more than one bureau.
- I am not paying someone for a promise to remove accurate negative information.

## FAQ

**Can I dispute a personal loan that is reported late by mistake?**
Yes. If the late status is inaccurate, identify the exact payment period, provide supporting records, and dispute with both the credit reporting company and the furnisher.

**Can I dispute a wrong personal-loan balance?**
Yes, if the balance is inaccurate. Save statements or payoff records that support the correct figure and make the dispute specific.

**What if the personal loan is not mine?**
Treat an unrecognized account as a possible identity-theft or mixed-file issue. Follow identity-theft recovery guidance rather than treating it only as an ordinary account-data error.

**Can accurate late payments be removed because they hurt my credit?**
Generally, no. CFPB guidance says accurate negative information generally cannot simply be removed. Duplicate, inaccurate, or identity-theft information is different.

**Will fixing an error increase my score?**
CashPath cannot guarantee that. Scoring effects depend on the entire credit file and the model used.

## Bottom Line

A personal-loan reporting error should be handled as an evidence problem, not a score-hacking exercise.

Identify the exact inaccurate field, preserve the report, collect records, dispute with both the credit reporting company and the furnisher, and verify the result. If the account is not yours, switch to identity-theft recovery steps. If the negative information is accurate, do not treat a dispute as a shortcut to deletion.
