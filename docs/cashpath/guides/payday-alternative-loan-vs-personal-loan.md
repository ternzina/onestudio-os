## Short Answer

A Payday Alternative Loan, or PAL, and a conventional personal loan can both be used to cover a relatively small cash need, but they are not the same product.

An official NCUA Payday Alternative Loan is a regulated small-dollar loan that a participating **federal credit union** may offer to eligible members. Federal rules create specific PAL I and PAL II structures, but not every federal credit union offers them and membership alone does not guarantee approval.

A conventional personal loan is credit offered by a bank, credit union, online lender, or other provider under that provider's own underwriting and product terms.

If both are realistically available, compare the actual disclosures side by side:

- how much you need;
- how much you can actually receive;
- whether you meet the institution's membership and eligibility rules;
- APR;
- application or origination fees;
- payment amount;
- number and timing of payments;
- total repayment;
- what happens after a missed payment; and
- whether taking the loan leaves enough room for rent, food, utilities, transportation, insurance, and existing debts.

Do not assume a PAL is automatically the cheapest option. Do not assume a personal loan will be available for a very small amount. The useful comparison is between two actual offers or one actual offer and a realistic alternative, not between marketing labels.

## What Is an NCUA Payday Alternative Loan?

Payday Alternative Loans are specific small-dollar loan programs that federal credit unions may offer under National Credit Union Administration rules.

The word **alternative** matters. A business cannot turn an ordinary high-cost loan into an official PAL simply by calling it a "payday alternative."

Current NCUA materials describe two federal-credit-union structures: PAL I and PAL II.

A consumer should first ask:

- Is this institution a federal credit union?
- Does it actually offer PAL I, PAL II, or another small-dollar product?
- What membership requirements apply?
- What are the loan's written terms?
- Is the product being described under the NCUA PAL rules, or is "payday alternative" only marketing language?

If the product is not an NCUA PAL, compare it under its own contract and applicable law rather than importing PAL rules into it.

## PAL I Has Specific Federal Parameters

NCUA consumer and regulatory materials describe PAL I as a structured small-dollar program for federal credit unions.

Current NCUA materials state that PAL I generally includes:

- loan amounts from $200 to $1,000;
- a term of one to six months;
- a requirement that the borrower have been a member of the federal credit union for at least one month before receiving the loan; and
- an application fee limited to the actual processing cost, up to $20.

The federal rule also contains safeguards around amortization, the number of PALs a borrower may receive within a period, and rollovers.

These are regulatory parameters, not a promise that a particular credit union will offer the maximum amount, approve an application, charge a specific fee, or make the loan immediately.

The credit union may impose lawful product requirements that are more restrictive than the outer federal rule.

## PAL II Is a Different Structure

PAL II was created as an additional federal-credit-union small-dollar option.

NCUA materials state that PAL II may generally be offered:

- in an amount up to $2,000;
- for a term of one to twelve months; and
- without the one-month membership waiting period that applies to PAL I.

Other PAL safeguards continue to matter, including full amortization and restrictions intended to prevent repeated rollover-style borrowing.

Do not treat PAL II as a guaranteed $2,000 loan. The federal rule describes what a qualifying product may permit. The individual credit union still decides whether it offers PAL II and whether a member satisfies its lawful criteria.

## The 28% PAL Figure Is a Regulatory Ceiling, Not a Market Quote

The NCUA's February 2026 interest-rate ceiling letter, modified July 9, 2026, says federal credit unions may charge up to **28%** on Payday Alternative Loans under the PAL rules. The general federal-credit-union loan-rate ceiling was extended through September 10, 2027.

That 28% figure should be read correctly.

It is a regulatory ceiling for qualifying PALs. It is **not**:

- a CashPath rate;
- a promise that a credit union will charge 28%;
- a statement that all PALs have the same APR;
- a personal-loan market average; or
- proof that a PAL is cheaper than another loan you are considering.

Use the APR and fees in the actual credit-union disclosure.

If a personal-loan provider gives you an offer, use that offer's APR and fees too. Then compare the same dollar need across both options.

## A Personal Loan Follows the Provider's Own Product Rules

A conventional personal loan does not use the NCUA PAL framework unless the loan itself is an eligible PAL issued by a federal credit union under those rules.

For a personal loan, important terms can include:

- minimum and maximum loan amount;
- permitted purpose;
- APR;
- interest rate;
- origination or other fees;
- net proceeds after any fee deducted from funding;
- scheduled payment;
- repayment term;
- prepayment terms;
- late or returned-payment terms; and
- credit inquiry and reporting practices.

Those terms vary by provider. CashPath's [Rates & Fees](/p/rates-fees) page explains the main cost fields to review.

CashPath does not set them and should not publish a universal personal-loan minimum, APR range, approval score, income threshold, or funding time unless a specific current provider disclosure supports it and the context requires it.

## Start With the Exact Cash Gap

Before comparing products, define the problem.

Write down:

- the exact bill or expense;
- the amount due;
- the due date;
- how much you can pay without borrowing;
- the remaining financing gap;
- when reliable income is expected; and
- the maximum scheduled payment your budget can absorb without borrowing again.

Suppose an essential expense is $900 and you can safely contribute $250 from current cash flow.

The financing gap is $650.

That does not mean you should automatically take a $1,000 PAL I, a $2,000 PAL II, or a larger personal loan. Those figures describe possible product boundaries, not the amount you need.

Compare the smallest realistic amount that solves the verified problem.

## Membership Availability Can Decide the Comparison Before Price Does

PALs are tied to federal credit unions.

That means the first practical question may be whether you are already eligible to use the institution offering the product.

Ask the credit union:

- Do you offer PAL I or PAL II today?
- Am I eligible to join this credit union?
- If I am already a member, does my membership duration satisfy the product's rules?
- Are there account or membership conditions connected to the loan?
- What documents are required?
- What amount would the institution actually consider?

Do not confuse **ability to join** with **approval for credit**. They are separate questions.

A credit union may have a lawful field of membership, and the product may have its own underwriting or account requirements. CashPath does not determine either.

## Compare Net Proceeds, Not Just the Stated Loan Amount

The amount shown on a loan offer is not always the amount that reaches your bank account.

For a conventional personal loan, a disclosed origination or other upfront fee may be deducted from proceeds depending on the product.

For PAL I, the NCUA permits an application fee tied to actual processing cost up to the regulatory cap described above.

Use the actual disclosure and write down:

- stated loan amount: $________
- application or origination fee: $________
- other disclosed upfront cost: $________
- net cash received: $________
- amount of the original bill still unpaid: $________

A loan that appears large enough on paper but leaves the household short after fees may not solve the problem.

## Compare APR and Total Repayment Together

APR is useful because it helps express the annualized cost of credit, but it should not be the only number you review.

For a short small-dollar loan, also record:

- finance charge in dollars;
- application or origination fee;
- each payment amount;
- number of payments;
- first payment date;
- final payment date; and
- total amount repaid.

The same APR can feel very different in a household budget depending on the term and payment schedule.

A lower scheduled payment can also hide a longer repayment period. A shorter period can create a payment that collides with essential expenses.

The right comparison uses both cost and cash flow.

## Repayment Structure Matters More Than the Product Name

NCUA PAL rules are designed around fully amortizing repayment rather than indefinite rollover-style extensions.

A conventional personal loan is also generally repaid through scheduled installments, but the exact schedule depends on the provider.

For both products, ask:

- How many payments are required?
- Are payments weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, or monthly?
- Is the first payment due before my next reliable income date?
- Does the payment fit after essential bills?
- Is there a balloon payment?
- Can the loan be renewed, refinanced, or replaced with another loan?
- What happens if I miss a payment?

Do not choose a loan because the phrase "small dollar" sounds manageable. A $200 payment can still be unaffordable if only $100 remains after necessary household expenses.

## PALs Are Not the Same as Payday Loans

The PAL program exists as a regulated federal-credit-union alternative to conventional payday lending.

That does not make every small credit-union loan a PAL, and it does not make every product marketed as a payday alternative an official PAL.

If you are also considering a payday loan, treat that as a separate comparison.

For an official PAL, use the federal credit union's PAL disclosure. For a payday product, use the lender's actual disclosure and applicable state/federal rules. Do not transfer one product's protections or limits to the other.

## PALs Are Also Different From Earned Wage Access

Earned Wage Access can involve access to wages already earned under a provider or employer program. A PAL is a loan issued under a federal-credit-union lending framework.

Those are different legal and economic structures.

If your decision is between borrowing and accessing earned wages, treat that as a separate comparison rather than forcing EWA into the PAL framework.

## Practical Example: A $700 Repair Gap

Assume a household has a verified $700 repair that cannot reasonably be postponed.

The consumer is a member of a federal credit union that says it offers a PAL, and the consumer also receives a personal-loan offer elsewhere.

Do not compare the two by the largest amount each institution is willing to lend.

Build this worksheet from the actual disclosures:

**PAL**

- amount offered: $________
- net proceeds: $________
- APR: ________
- application fee: $________
- payment amount: $________
- number of payments: ________
- total repayment: $________
- first payment date: ________

**Personal loan**

- amount offered: $________
- net proceeds: $________
- APR: ________
- origination or other fee: $________
- payment amount: $________
- number of payments: ________
- total repayment: $________
- first payment date: ________

Then ask:

- Which option provides at least the $700 actually needed?
- Which payment schedule fits reliable income?
- Which option has the lower total cost under the actual disclosures?
- Does either option force the household to borrow more than necessary?
- Would either payment make rent, food, utilities, insurance, or transportation unaffordable?

The example does not state or imply that either loan will be available.

## Check Non-Loan Alternatives Before Adding Debt

A small cash gap may sometimes be solved without a new loan.

Depending on the expense, consider checking:

- whether the biller can move the due date;
- whether the bill can be split into payments;
- whether a utility, medical provider, landlord, or service provider has a hardship process;
- whether an employer offers legitimate assistance;
- whether a nonprofit or local assistance program applies to the expense; or
- whether part of the expense can be delayed safely.

Availability varies, and some arrangements may have their own costs or consequences.

The point is not to assume a free alternative exists. It is to check before paying interest or fees on a new debt. CashPath's [Responsible Lending](/p/responsible-lending) page provides a general repayment-fit checklist.

## Small-Dollar Warning Signs

Pause before accepting any loan if:

- approval is guaranteed before the provider reviews your information;
- you are asked to pay money upfront to "unlock" a loan;
- the lender or credit union cannot clearly explain APR or total repayment;
- repayment dates are unclear;
- the payment requires another loan to keep up with normal expenses;
- the company uses "PAL" language but cannot explain whether the product is an NCUA PAL from a federal credit union;
- you cannot identify the actual creditor;
- the agreement is materially different from what was advertised; or
- you are pressured to sign before reading the disclosures.

Urgency is a reason to slow down the contract review, not skip it.

## Decision Checklist

Before choosing between a PAL and a personal loan, confirm that:

- I know the exact amount I need.
- I checked whether the expense can be reduced, delayed, or placed on a payment plan.
- I verified whether the credit union actually offers PAL I or PAL II.
- I understand any membership requirement.
- I know the amount I would actually receive after fees.
- I know the APR.
- I know all disclosed fees.
- I know the payment amount and schedule.
- I know the total repayment.
- I know what happens after a missed payment.
- I am not assuming the NCUA 28% ceiling is the rate I will receive.
- I am not assuming a PAL is automatically cheaper than a personal loan.
- I am not borrowing substantially more than the verified need without a clear reason.
- The payment leaves room for essential household expenses.

## FAQ

**Is a Payday Alternative Loan the same as a payday loan?**

No. An official PAL is a regulated federal-credit-union small-dollar product offered under NCUA rules. A payday loan is a different credit product governed by its own contract and applicable law. Do not assume a product is an official PAL merely because its marketing uses the words "payday alternative."

**Does every credit union offer PALs?**

No. NCUA rules permit federal credit unions to offer PAL products, but they do not require every federal credit union to do so. State-chartered credit unions may offer other small-dollar products under different authority.

**Can I get a PAL as soon as I join a federal credit union?**

PAL I generally has at least a one-month membership requirement under current NCUA rules. PAL II was designed without that one-month requirement. The institution must still offer the product, and membership does not guarantee loan approval.

**Are PALs capped at 28% APR?**

Current NCUA guidance allows federal credit unions to charge up to 28% on qualifying PALs. That is a regulatory ceiling, not a statement that every PAL carries 28% APR or that CashPath offers that rate.

**Is a PAL always cheaper than a personal loan?**

No universal answer is safe. Compare the actual APR, fees, payment schedule, amount received, and total repayment for the options available to you.

**Does CashPath offer PALs?**

No. CashPath is a personal-loan request and referral service, not a federal credit union or lender. CashPath does not issue PALs or determine credit-union membership or eligibility.

## Bottom Line

A Payday Alternative Loan can be a useful small-dollar option to investigate when a participating federal credit union offers one and the consumer meets its requirements.

But the label alone does not decide the better option.

Verify that the product is actually a PAL, understand whether PAL I or PAL II rules apply, and compare the real disclosure with any personal-loan offer using the same fields: amount needed, net proceeds, APR, fees, payment timing, total repayment, and household affordability.

The best comparison is the one that solves the verified cash gap without relying on a larger loan, a repeated borrowing cycle, or assumptions about approval or price.
