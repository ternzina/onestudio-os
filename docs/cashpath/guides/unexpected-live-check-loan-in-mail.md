# Unexpected Check in the Mail? How to Tell If It Is a Live Check Loan

**Important:** CashPath is not a lender and cannot verify whether a mailed check is genuine or tell you whether to accept a credit offer. If a check is a live-check loan, the sender's agreement and disclosures control the terms. Verify the sender independently before sharing personal information or depositing funds.

## Short Answer

An unexpected check in the mail is not automatically free money, and it is not automatically a scam.

It could be a refund or payment that is legitimately owed to you. It could be a fake-check scam. Or it could be a **live check loan**, which the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau describes as an unsolicited or prescreened loan offer.

With a legitimate live-check loan, depositing or cashing the check can be treated as accepting the loan under the terms disclosed in the mailing. That is why the safest order is:

- **1.** identify what the check is;
- **2.** verify who sent it;
- **3.** read the complete disclosures;
- **4.** compare alternatives; and
- **5.** only then decide whether to deposit or cash it.

Do not deposit an unfamiliar check first and investigate later.

## What Is a Live Check Loan?

A live check loan is a credit offer delivered as a negotiable check.

Unlike a normal personal-loan application, you may not have asked for the offer. The check can arrive because a company used prescreening or another permitted marketing process to identify potential recipients.

The CFPB says a live-check mailing should come with information that allows the recipient to understand the offered credit, including the APR, fees, payment schedule, loan agreement, privacy notice, opt-out information, and contact information for the sender.

The most important practical detail is that the check is not simply a coupon. If the offer is genuine, endorsing and depositing or cashing it can create a repayment obligation.

## First Decide What Kind of Check You Received

Before focusing on loan terms, identify the category.

A legitimate **refund or payment** may come from a company, government agency, insurer, employer, merchant, or other party that actually owes you money.

A **fake check** may look convincing but is sent to get you to send money, buy gift cards, return an "overpayment," or disclose personal information.

A **live check loan** is an unsolicited credit offer with repayment terms attached.

The printed word "preapproved" does not prove that the check is legitimate. A scammer can copy financial language and branding.

## Do Not Use the Phone Number on a Suspicious Mailing as Your Only Verification

If you do not recognize the sender, verify the organization independently.

Look for the company's official website through a trusted source, check whether the legal business name matches the mailing, and confirm contact information independently before discussing personal data.

Be especially cautious if someone pressures you to act immediately, asks for a fee before you can receive supposed loan funds, requests payment by gift card or cryptocurrency, or tells you to return part of a check after depositing it.

A legitimate-looking check can still be fraudulent.

## What to Read Before Depositing a Live Check

If the mailing appears to be a genuine loan offer, do not judge it by the check amount alone.

Review:

- the name of the creditor or provider;
- the amount being offered;
- the APR;
- any finance charges or other disclosed fees;
- how often payments are due;
- the amount and number of scheduled payments;
- the total repayment shown in the disclosure, where applicable;
- late-payment or returned-payment provisions;
- prepayment language;
- the date by which the offer must be accepted, if stated;
- privacy and information-sharing notices; and
- instructions for declining or opting out of future prescreened offers.

If any important term is missing or confusing, do not deposit the check until you understand it.

## Why the APR Matters More Than the Size of the Check

A check can feel immediate: a fixed dollar amount is sitting in your hand.

The repayment obligation is less visible.

APR is designed to help communicate the annualized cost of credit. It can be more informative than looking only at an interest-rate headline because certain finance charges can affect the APR.

The scheduled payment also does not tell you the whole cost. A lower payment stretched over a longer period can keep you in debt longer and may increase total repayment.

CashPath does not set the APR, fees, payment, or term on a live-check offer. Those numbers come from the actual creditor's disclosures.

## Example: The Decision Before the Deposit

Suppose you receive a $2,000 check labeled as a preapproved loan.

You have not applied for a loan.

Before doing anything with the check, ask:

- Who sent it?
- Is the company real?
- Is this a loan rather than a refund?
- What APR and fees are disclosed?
- What payment is required and for how long?
- What would the total repayment be?
- Is there a less expensive way to cover the expense?
- Can your budget absorb the required payment without crowding out rent, utilities, food, transportation, or existing debt?

The decision should happen **before** the deposit, because deposit may be the act that accepts the loan.

## What If You Do Not Want the Loan?

If you confirm that the mailing is an unwanted live-check loan offer and you do not intend to accept it, the CFPB recommends securely destroying the check so another person cannot use it.

Shredding is safer than placing an intact negotiable check in the trash.

The CFPB also explains that consumers have rights under federal law to opt out of certain prescreened credit and insurance offers for five years or permanently. The official opt-out process is available through OptOutPrescreen.

Opting out does not cancel an existing debt or reverse a check you already deposited. It is about future prescreened offers.

## What If You Already Deposited the Check?

Do not assume you can simply return the principal later with no additional obligation.

Locate the agreement and disclosures that came with the check and contact the actual creditor using independently verified contact information.

Ask for:

- confirmation that the account was opened;
- the current balance;
- the payment schedule;
- the due date;
- the APR and applicable fees;
- any cancellation or rescission rights that actually apply to the agreement;
- payoff instructions if you want to repay early; and
- written confirmation of what happens next.

Do not assume every personal loan has a federal three-day cancellation period. Cancellation rights depend on the product, transaction, contract, and applicable law.

## What If the Check Looks Fake?

Do not deposit it as a test.

A bank can make funds appear available before a fraudulent check is finally identified. If you send money onward and the check later fails, you may be responsible for the money you sent.

Do not send a "processing fee" or return an alleged overpayment.

If the mailing appears fraudulent, preserve enough information to make a report, then use official government resources such as ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If you believe personal information has already been stolen or misused, IdentityTheft.gov provides a recovery process.

## A Live-Check Safety Checklist

Before accepting an unsolicited check:

- I know whether it is a refund/payment, live-check loan, or suspicious check.
- I verified the sender independently.
- I found the creditor's legal identity and contact information.
- I read the APR and fee disclosures.
- I know the payment amount, frequency, and repayment period.
- I understand what act constitutes acceptance.
- I compared the cost with other available options.
- The required payment fits my budget.
- I have not sent money or personal data in response to unexplained pressure.
- If I do not want the offer, I will destroy the check securely.

## FAQ

**Is every preapproved check a loan?**
No. An unexpected check can be a legitimate payment, a live-check loan offer, or a fake check. Identify the source and read the accompanying documents before acting.

**Does depositing a live-check loan mean I accepted it?**
The CFPB states that cashing or depositing a legitimate live check can be treated as acceptance of the offered loan terms. Read the agreement before depositing.

**Is a live-check loan automatically a scam?**
No. Live-check loans can be legitimate credit offers. But fake-check scams can imitate loan offers, so independent verification matters.

**Can CashPath verify a check for me?**
No. CashPath is not the creditor, bank, or check issuer and cannot authenticate a mailed check.

**How can I reduce future prescreened offers?**
The CFPB points consumers to the official OptOutPrescreen process for opting out of certain prescreened credit and insurance offers.

## Bottom Line

An unexpected check should trigger verification, not urgency.

If it is a live-check loan, depositing it can create a real debt. Confirm the sender, understand the APR, fees, payment schedule, and total repayment, compare alternatives, and decide before you endorse the check.

If the check is suspicious, do not send money or personal information to "unlock" it.
